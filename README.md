# Daily Command

Product Vision

Build the AI workspace for everyday life and work — a personal operating layer that helps people communicate, plan, organize, and get things done across their apps without needing to prompt like a power user.

The product is not another chatbot. It is a context-aware action workspace: it understands the user’s email, calendar, documents, notes, tasks, preferences, and current goals, then turns messy daily intent into completed work with the user in control.

One-line vision

Cursor for everyday work: an AI workspace that understands your context, coordinates your apps, and helps you finish daily tasks with reviewable actions.

Mission

Help people spend less time managing digital busywork and more time doing what matters.

The product should remove the repetitive friction of modern life: drafting replies, planning days, preparing for meetings, organizing tasks, creating itineraries, following up, rewriting documents, and switching between apps.

Core belief

People do not want “more AI chat.”

They want:

less copy-pasting, less remembering, less switching, less planning from scratch, and fewer unfinished loops.

The product wins by turning AI from a conversation partner into a dependable personal workflow system.

Target user

The first users are people who already use AI but still feel friction moving between tools.

Primary early adopters:

 Students managing school, applications, deadlines, and communication.

 Founders and freelancers juggling clients, email, meetings, and planning.

 Busy professionals who spend too much time in inbox, calendar, docs, and chat.

 Creators or operators who need fast writing, coordination, and organization.

They are not necessarily technical. They are digitally overloaded.

The problem

Today, users rely on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Gmail, Calendar, Docs, Notes, Notion, WhatsApp, Slack, browsers, and task apps separately.

The user has to act as the glue:

 Find the right context.

 Copy it into AI.

 Explain the task.

 Ask for the right format.

 Move the output back to another app.

 Remember the follow-up.

 Repeat the same process tomorrow.

This is exhausting.

The real problem is not lack of intelligence.
The real problem is lack of connected execution.

The product

A personal AI workspace with four core layers:

1. Context Layer

The product connects to the user’s apps and builds a structured understanding of their world.

It knows:

 Calendar events.

 Email threads.

 Important contacts.

 Current projects.

 Documents and notes.

 Deadlines.

 Writing style.

 Preferences.

 Open loops.

 Travel and planning habits.

The context must be visible and editable. The user should always know what the AI knows and what it used.

2. Workflow Layer

Instead of starting from a blank chat, users choose or trigger workflows.

Core workflows:

Reply Zero: triage inbox, draft replies, suggest follow-ups.

Plan My Day: turn calendar, messages, and tasks into a realistic daily plan.

Follow-Up Tracker: track promises, unanswered messages, and pending actions.

Meeting Prep: summarize context, prepare questions, draft notes.

Write This Properly: turn rough thoughts into polished emails, docs, messages, or applications.

Trip Builder: create editable itineraries using preferences, dates, budget, and calendar constraints.

Weekly Review: summarize the week, unfinished work, and next priorities.

3. Action Layer

The AI does useful work across apps, but with user approval.

It can:

 Draft emails.

 Suggest replies.

 Create calendar events.

 Reschedule meetings.

 Create docs.

 Update tasks.

 Summarize threads.

 Generate itineraries.

 Prepare meeting notes.

 Send follow-ups.

 Organize information.

The user approves important actions before anything external happens.

4. Trust Layer

Trust is the product.

Every AI action should show:

 What context was used.

 What the AI plans to do.

 What will change.

 Where the output will go.

 The final draft before sending.

 Undo/history where possible.

The user should feel assisted, not replaced or tricked.

Product principles

1. Workflow before chat

Chat is available, but it should not be the main product structure.

The product should feel like:

“Here are the things you need to handle.”

Not:

“Type something and hope the AI helps.”

2. Approval before autonomy

Start with reviewable drafts and suggested actions. Earn trust before automation.

3. Context beats prompting

The less the user has to explain, the better the product is.

4. Show your work

The AI should cite the email, calendar event, note, or document it used.

5. Opinionated defaults

Normal users do not want to configure models, prompts, agents, and chains. Give them useful modes:

 Fast.

 Smart.

 Careful.

 Creative.

 Private.

Advanced users can access model controls later.

6. Reduce app-switching

The product should become the place where daily work is reviewed, drafted, planned, and approved.

7. Memory must be controllable

User context should be transparent, editable, and deletable.

The experience

A user opens the product in the morning.

It says:

“You have 3 messages that need replies, 2 meetings today, 1 thing you promised to send, and a free 90-minute block this afternoon. Here’s a suggested plan.”

The user clicks Handle replies.

The AI shows:

 Email summary.

 Suggested response.

 Tone explanation.

 Follow-up suggestion.

 Approval button.

The user approves or edits.

Then the product says:

“Your 2 PM meeting has no prep notes. Want a quick brief?”

It prepares:

 Who is attending.

 What was discussed last time.

 Related documents.

 Suggested questions.

 Draft agenda.

This is the product experience: proactive, contextual, reviewable execution.

MVP vision

The MVP should focus on one painful daily loop:

Inbox + Calendar + Writing

The first version should help users:

 Understand what matters today.

 Draft replies.

 Schedule or reschedule events.

 Track follow-ups.

 Write polished messages and documents.

 Convert messy thoughts into useful outputs.

Do not start with every use case. Start by becoming excellent at communication and planning.

MVP feature set

Must-have

 Gmail/Outlook integration.

 Google/Outlook Calendar integration.

 Daily briefing.

 Inbox triage.

 AI reply drafting.

 Calendar-aware scheduling suggestions.

 Follow-up detection.

 Writing composer.

 User tone profile.

 Approval flow before sending or scheduling.

Nice-to-have

 Google Docs/Drive integration.

 Browser extension.

 Task app integration.

 Voice note capture.

 Itinerary planner.

 Multi-model selection.

 Workflow template marketplace.

Long-term vision

Become the user’s personal operations system.

Over time, the product expands from communication into full daily coordination:

 Email.

 Calendar.

 Docs.

 Notes.

 Tasks.

 Browser.

 Travel.

 Shopping research.

 Forms.

 Personal finance organization.

 School/work planning.

 Family coordination.

The eventual product can answer:

“What should I do today?”

And then help execute it.

It can answer:

“What am I forgetting?”

And show open loops.

It can answer:

“Can you handle this?”

And produce a clear, reviewable action plan.

Category

This is not just a chatbot category.

The category is:

Personal AI Workspace

or

AI Personal Operations System

or

AI Workflow Assistant for Everyday Work

The best category language is probably:

Personal AI Workspace

It is broad enough for the future, but concrete enough for users to understand.

Competitive positioning

Compared to ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini:

 They are general-purpose AI assistants.

 This is a workflow-first personal workspace.

Compared to Alexa/Siri:

 They are command assistants.

 This is an app-connected execution assistant.

Compared to Notion AI/Copilot:

 They live inside one workspace.

 This works across the user’s actual apps.

Compared to Zapier:

 Zapier automates rigid workflows.

 This handles flexible, messy human workflows.

Product promise

Your AI workspace knows what you’re working on, helps you decide what matters, drafts the work, coordinates your apps, and lets you approve everything before it acts.

North Star

The product should reduce the amount of time users spend on digital coordination.

Possible North Star metric:

Number of user-approved tasks completed per week.

Supporting metrics:

 Replies drafted.

 Follow-ups captured.

 Calendar actions completed.

 Daily briefings opened.

 Workflows completed.

 Time from intent to approved action.

 Percentage of AI suggestions accepted.

 Number of app switches avoided.

Strategic wedge

Start with:

“The AI assistant that clears your inbox and plans your day.”

Then expand to:

“The AI workspace for everyday communication, planning, and personal operations.”

This gives you a focused entry point and a much bigger long-term product vision.
